Deep Solar Activity & Cosmogenic Spikes

Beyond the familiar 11-year sunspot heartbeat lie multi-century oscillations like the Gleissberg, Suess, and Hallstatt cycles. In ancient tree rings and polar ice cores, our planet preserves signatures of extreme Solar Particle Events (Miyake Events) tens to hundreds of times more energetic than the 1859 Carrington storm.

What is a True "Micronova"?

Identified by ESO's VLT and NASA's TESS in 2022, micronovae are localized surface thermonuclear detonations on accreting white dwarfs in binary systems (~10³⁹ ergs). Our isolated G-type Sun cannot produce a thermonuclear micronova, but it produces violent magnetic superflares and extreme proton storms.

Energy Scale Comparison

Solar Fluctuation vs Micronova vs Superstorms

Energy release measured in ergs (logarithmic comparison). Note the distinct physical jump between coronal mass ejections, cosmogenic Miyake particle storms, and actual thermonuclear white-dwarf micronovae.

May 2024 Geomagnetic Storm (G5 / X8.7) ~10³² ergs
Carrington Event (1859 CME / White-light Flare) ~10³²·⁵ ergs
774–775 CE Miyake Event (Extreme Solar Proton Event) ~10³⁴ ergs (~50× Carrington SPE)
12,350 BCE Event (14.3k yr BP Pine Radiocarbon Spike) ~10³⁴·⁵ ergs (Largest Known SPE)
Accreting White Dwarf Micronova (TESS/VLT Discovery) ~10³⁹ ergs (Thermonuclear runaway)

*A true micronova is ~100,000 to 1,000,000× more energetic than the largest solar proton storm in Earth's prehistoric record. It requires an accreting degenerate electron surface and a companion star.
